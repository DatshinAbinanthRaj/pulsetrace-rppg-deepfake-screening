# Evaluation

## What was measured

All numbers below come from **synthetic clips** generated by `pulsetrace/synthetic.py`. The faces are drawn procedurally, so the ground truth (real/fake and true heart rate) is known exactly.

| Run | Command | Clips | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synthetic benchmark (files, MJPG-encoded) | `pulsetrace synth --per-kind 5 --seed 500` then `pulsetrace evaluate` | 15 real, 15 fake (5 no-pulse, 5 incoherent, 5 flicker) | accuracy 1.00, ROC-AUC 1.00, real-flagged-as-fake 0.00, BPM MAE 0.27 ([report](results/synthetic_benchmark.md)) |
| Pulse-strength × noise sweep | `pulsetrace stress --clips 2` | 96 in-memory clips, 8 cells | 1.00 in every cell down to a 0.075 % colour change ([table](results/stress_sweep.md)) |
| Head-motion check | `python examples/motion_stress.py` | 18 clips, motion 8–30 px | 1.00 in every cell ([table](results/motion_stress.md)) |

## How to read these numbers

**They show that the pipeline is wired correctly. They do not show how well it detects real deepfakes.** In particular:

1. The synthetic face has flat, untextured skin. On a real face, head motion and lighting move shadows and texture through the ROIs, which is the main source of rPPG error. That is why the motion check stayed perfect: it isn't a realistic test of motion.
2. Per-pixel sensor noise is independent, so averaging thousands of ROI pixels removes it. Real video noise is spatially correlated and shaped by compression.
3. The fake clips model three failure modes (no pulse, independent regional pulses, frame flicker). A generator that copies a coherent pulse from a source video (reenactment that preserves skin colour) would pass as real. This is a known blind spot of pulse-based detection.
4. Thresholds were set by hand and checked on synthetic data only.

Public testing points the same way: in the BBC's 2023 trial of Intel FakeCatcher, most fakes were caught, but several real clips were flagged as fake, and pixelated video made the blood-flow signal harder to read.

## How to evaluate on real data

1. Get a licensed dataset with real and manipulated face videos (for example FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF or DFDC). Follow each dataset's access terms. None are bundled here.
2. Write a manifest CSV:
   ```csv
   path,label
   videos/real/000.mp4,real
   videos/fake/000_003.mp4,fake
   ```
3. Run `pulsetrace evaluate manifest.csv --out results/real_eval --method pos`.
4. Read `report.md`. Pay most attention to **real_flagged_as_fake_rate** (false accusations) and **coverage** (how often the tool abstains).
5. Open `review_queue.csv`, label the queued clips, and re-tune `AnalysisConfig` thresholds and weights on a held-out split. Never tune on the test split.

## Metrics reported

| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| coverage | share of clips given a definite verdict (not NEEDS_REVIEW / INCONCLUSIVE) |
| accuracy_on_decided | accuracy over decided clips only |
| fake_precision / fake_recall / fake_f1 | "positive" = flagged as fake |
| real_flagged_as_fake_rate | false-positive rate on real clips: the costly error |
| roc_auc_score | threshold-free ranking quality of the 0–1 score |
| bpm_mae | heart-rate error on real clips with known BPM |
